# Where stories could go next

Interviews give a person room to look back on their life. Once there is enough material, Witness could help them explore different ways of telling it — and find people who recognise something of their own experience in it.

## An interview you can watch

Turn a reviewed story into a full video interview with a virtual host. The narrator chooses the interviewer, the setting, and the tone: a quiet conversation in a beautiful location, a studio interview, or a TV-show format. The style could be dramatic, humorous, warm, or documentary.

The narrator reviews the script before rendering and can change what is included. The visual style can change without adding events or quotes that were never part of the story. Fictional hosts and environments are the starting point for exploration.

Muse Spark could help build the script and connect scenes to interview answers. Muse Image could explore the host, set, and storyboard. Voice synthesis, avatars, and finished video need their own technology choice; image generation alone does not create a video interview.

## A game through a life

Turn selected memories into a playable story: explore places, meet people from the narrator's account, and move through turning points. Let the narrator choose the episodes and style of play.

Keep remembered events distinguishable from fictional branches. If the player explores “what if?”, make it clear that this is an imagined alternative rather than something that happened.

A text-based branching story would be the first experiment. Muse Spark could help organise the narrative and choices; visuals and the game engine would be selected for the format.

## A mini-series

Shape a longer interview into short episodes, each following a period, relationship, or turning point. The narrator chooses the tone and visual style and reviews the episode plan, script, and included memories.

Keep links from scenes to approved answers so the person can check how their experience has been adapted. Script planning, storyboards, audio, and final rendering are separate steps.

## A globe of shared experiences

A globe sits beside a list of events from your life. Select an event — moving abroad, a first concert, starting over — and places light up where other people have shared a similar experience.

Selecting a marker opens related stories. People can choose whether they want to connect. The globe is about shared experiences across places, not tracking where people are now.

The narrator can confirm the event's topic and place before using it for discovery. A specific shared event, such as the same concert, is different from a broad similarity, such as attending a first concert. Filters could include event, theme, and time period.

Only deliberately shared stories appear. Use city-level or broader locations selected by their authors, not precise private addresses. Show why a story matched and let its author remove it from discovery.

The [Connect preview](connect.md) uses fictional stories and simple topic matching to test the interaction. Event extraction could later use Muse Spark with narrator confirmation; globe rendering and location lookup need separate tools.

## What comes first

The interview, answer review, editable story, source links, and export form the first build. These future formats should grow from stories people have had a chance to review and make their own.
