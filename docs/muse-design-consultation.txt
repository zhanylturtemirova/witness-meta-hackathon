## Witness redesign brief

Goal: replace generic slogans and decoration with a calm tool that does four things well: ask one good question, keep what was said, help shape it, let the reader return.

Global language rule: name concrete actions. Say "Answer, skip, or change it later," not promises about legacy or self-discovery. Remove starbursts, vague 3-step diagrams, and text-card "videos."

### 1. Home

One screen, one question. Layout:

- Top: today's memory question, specific and small, e.g. "What did Sunday mornings sound like where you grew up?" with date. One "Ask me something else" control.
- Middle: two equal choices: "Record" (max 2 min, with transcript for review) and "Write." Both go to the same draft.
- Below: two lists only: "Unfinished" (question + first line + date) and "Reading" (finished stories, progress kept).

No streaks, points, or "complete your story" banners.

### 2. Connect preview

Keep as a separate, clearly labelled preview using fictional stories only. Real profiles, messaging, and publishing stay future work.

- Visual: full-screen dark space, one photoreal Earth, slow drift, no starfield clutter.
- Interaction: timeline of fictional shared events. Selecting one event lights 2–4 markers in sequence, draws one arc at a time, then stops. Never animate all arcs at once.
- Reading: max two short excerpts per event, each with theme and coarse place only ("coastal Portugal"), no exact pin. "Read more" opens text, not a profile.
- Location: opt-in, coarse, revocable, off by default. Store city/region text only. Say so on the screen.

### 3. Interviewer vs editor

Two separate Muse Spark roles, different prompts and UI:

- Interviewer: asks one follow-up from the last 2–3 answers. Can be skipped. Never summarises, never labels feelings, never suggests a diagnosis or life lesson.
- Editor: works only from saved answer versions. Proposed revisions show: old sentence, new sentence, source answer IDs. Narrator can keep, change, or reject each one. Editing an answer marks linked passages "Check again."

Faithfulness rule: keep "I don't remember," approximations, and pauses. No invented names, dates, or quotes.

### 4. Notifications

Opt-in only, off by default. Two types only:

- A gentle new question, max 1/day.
- A saved passage the narrator starred, max 2/week.

Settings: days, quiet hours (default 21:00–08:00), pause for 7/30 days, one-tap off. No streaks, no "You missed," no counts of unanswered questions.

### 5. Moving image

No photoreal fake autobiography. Offer only:

- Illustrated stills for approved scenes (one image per scene, reviewed).
- Short 2D scene adaptations: slow pan/dissolve over illustrations, narrator's own recorded or uploaded narration, subtitles from approved text.

Label each film "Illustrated version." Do not claim Meta APIs generate TTS or moving video; those capabilities must be verified separately. Plan currently on human narration + illustration only.

### 6. Publisher licensing

Separate from sharing with family/friends. Plan:

- No publisher access by default.
- Explicit second permission: what excerpt, who may see it, for how long.
- Narrator approves the exact text and illustration before any handover, and can withdraw. Keep an audit line: what, to whom, when.

## Visual directions

Listening room: warm off-white/paper, one serif for reading + plain sans for controls, large tap targets, visible waveform while recording, generous margins. Feels private and voice-first. Risk: can look flat if over-minimal.

World of memories: dark space Connect, Earth as focus, thin warm arcs, restrained type. Feels cinematic and human when excerpts lead. Risk: cold and spectacular if applied everywhere.

Recommendation: combine by place. Use Listening room for home, interview, reading, and editor. Reserve dark space only for Connect. Shared thread: same reading serif, same warm accent for "your words," same plain buttons. The contrast then means something: light = private work, dark = shared world preview.

## Prompts and evals before fine-tuning

No fine-tuning yet. Small, private data and quality problems are prompt problems first. For each task below, write prompt + 10–20 fictional examples + checks:

1. Next interview question; 2. clarification request; 3. life-event grouping; 4. passage draft with source IDs; 5. editor revision; 6. excerpt choice for Connect.

Evals: source IDs exist, no new facts, uncertainty kept, skip works, failure keeps answers. Consider other models or tuning only if a named check keeps failing.

## Implementation sequence

1. Home with one question, record/write, two lists. 2. Split interviewer/editor prompts and side-by-side review. 3. Restrained Connect: sequential arcs, two excerpts, coarse opt-in place. 4. Notification settings with quiet hours. 5. Illustrated 2D film path with approvals. 6. Licensing as explicit permission + approval log.
